Fairness Across Traditions
My working definition

What is fair?

Fairness is taking no advantage at the expense of any affected being1 — or of the living systems all beings depend on2 — that they cannot or could not have taken in return; making no distinction between beings that you would reject for yourself3; and where such advantage has already been taken, restoring what could not be defended4.

1

The reciprocity test

An advantage is unfair if the other side could never take it back.

Meanwhile · China's Tiangong
Excluded from the ISS, China built its own station
  • One owner: China. Core module 2021; complete Nov 2022 (3 modules, T-shape).
  • Open on China's terms: first foreign astronaut, from Pakistan, planned for 2026 (deal signed Feb 2025).
  • Result: two stations, two blocs. Exclusion created a rival system, the split Prof. Maracke warns about for AI.

Sources: Space.com · Planetary Society · Profit (Pakistan Today)

Lesson for AI: share rights by contribution, but put custody on neutral ground (Prof. Maracke).

Sources: NASA: IGA signing (1998) · ESA: ISS legal framework · ESA ECSL: Art. 5 · ESA: patents (Art. 21) · CRS: criminal jurisdiction (Art. 22) · Wikipedia: usage shares (ESA's 8.3% confirmed by ESA) · Caltech: Wolf Amendment (2011) · CSIS: its limits · NASA: ops to 2030, Russia to at least 2028, deorbit vehicle

Three asks

1A multi-stakeholder charter with federated nodes, not one repository.
2A binding non-discrimination rule, so no single national order can switch off defence.
3Keep unpublished vulnerabilities on neutral ground, governed by many countries.
Through my fairness definition
  • Reciprocity: allies were cut off and could not cut back. That is an advantage they cannot take in return.
  • Self-test: would the US accept another country switching off its defences?
  • Restoration: federated, neutral custody gives back what single-country control took away.
  • East–West link: federation treats security as interdependence, close to the relational view in Research Part 1.

But direct tests · 35 Japan–US studies
  • Americans were not less collectivist than Japanese or Koreans (Oyserman 2002 meta-analysis).
  • Individualism rose ~12% worldwide, 1960–2011, in 78 countries (Santos 2017).
Verdict: clear on thinking style (holistic vs analytic); weak on stated values; shrinking over time.

      What the critics say

      Show 4 critiques
      Averages aren't peopleA country score says little about any one person (ecological fallacy).
      Weak data baseHofstede's scores come from IBM staff surveys, 1967–73 (McSweeney 2002).
      The West is the odd oneMost psychology samples are Western and unusual, not the human norm (Henrich et al. 2010).
      Blocks hide diversity"East" was built as the West's Other (Said 1978); "Asian values" served rulers (Sen 1997).
      Bottom lineLand (rice vs wheat, frontiers) and history (family rules, wealth) explain part of the pattern. The evidence is mostly correlational and works best inside countries. Treat "individualist West, relational East" as a tendency on some measures, not a fixed essence.
